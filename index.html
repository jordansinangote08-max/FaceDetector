    7
    Each pose is guided and quality-checked, then stored twice — once as seen and once mirrored — so the profile covers both sides of the face. Seven poses is a good start; ten is noticeably stronger.

    Why the guidance matters. Facegate refuses blurry, backlit, too-small or badly-angled samples instead of quietly storing them. A bad sample is worse than no sample: it is the usual reason a system starts calling one person by another person’s name.

    How it stays accurate over time. Each capture becomes a 128-number descriptor — a mathematical fingerprint, not a photo. When someone is recognised with very high confidence and good image quality, Facegate quietly keeps that fresh descriptor too, so the profile follows hair, weight, glasses and age as they change.

    Vault

    The backup stays encrypted with this same passphrase, so it is useless to anyone who copies it.

    What honest accuracy looks like. No face recognition system is 100% correct — not this one, not the ones on phones. On a small roster in decent light, expect roughly 97–99% with the lookalike guard on. It gets worse with harsh backlight, heavy motion blur, masks, faces far from the camera, and identical twins. Treat a match as strong evidence, never as proof.

    Where your data lives. Descriptors are encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key stretched from your passphrase (PBKDF2-SHA256, 310,000 rounds) and stored only in this browser. There is no account, no server, no upload. Someone who steals the device still faces the encryption — but keep the passphrase strong, because that is the whole lock.
